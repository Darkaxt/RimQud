What the clothing pipeline actually does

This review uses existing artifacts. No new clothing generation, fitting, or game test was performed to create this page. The profile is shown throughout at the same canvas size; stage 12 is an annotated proposal.

Important correction: late-mask-v3 does not yet fully implement an intact-cloth-first workflow. Closed holes are filled before stretching, but the stretch still reuses an older cutoff-derived alpha contour, and some hidden material is added afterward. Front/back textures remain incomplete. A clean assembled image does not prove a complete retained garment.

Proposed simpler fitting change

Start with three bands defined by clothing landmarks, not equal thirds of the whole transparent canvas. Keep the lower robe at its chosen placement and scale; adjust the shoulder/sleeve region, and use the middle region to connect them continuously. The body and its visibility mask remain fixed. These bands have not been implemented.

BandIntended role
Upper: collar, shoulders and sleevesAdjust width/height and placement to the fixed body, with explicit cuff and neckline anchors.
Middle: sleeve exit to lower torsoTransition between the upper transform and the frozen lower region; shared boundaries prevent seams.
Lower: skirt and hemPreserve the selected lower silhouette, contact relationship and detail.

A 3-by-3 grid can later separate left sleeve, torso and right sleeve if three bands cannot satisfy the anchors. Independent bitmap tiles would introduce seams; adjacent regions must share their boundary coordinates. One coordinate map should sample the original intact cloth once.

This can limit unnecessary distortion, but it cannot restore missing fabric or repair a sleeve line already drawn in the wrong place. Those remain separate source-art issues. Preserve the full cloth first; apply the fixed-body exposure mask only after the complete transform.
